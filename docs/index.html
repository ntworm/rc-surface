1.0

RC Surface

Any device with a browser becomes a performance controller for Ableton Live. Your own machine serves the surface over the local network and the phone just opens a link — nothing to install on it, no account, no internet.

Works in landscape. Six performance tabs plus the MAP overlay: twelve pads, eight knobs, eight faders, two XY pads and three sensor inputs. This sheet is the operator’s reference for build 1.0.0.

Published releases Source & licence

New in 1.0

  • Config Mode (CFG)

    Every pad, knob, fader, toggle, stutter and XY pad keeps its own mode, shape, subdivision, swing and physics overrides. Open CFG from the phone header or right-click a control on the desktop mapping panel to open a per-instance menu. Settings persist per control in localStorage under ableton-rc:control_config.

  • LFO waveform preview

    The SYNC settings modal draws the active LFO shape (sine, triangle, ramp up, ramp down, square) so you can see the curve you are about to push to Live, including the locked 32-beat subdivision and the per-control ceiling (sine tops at 4 Hz, square at 12 Hz).

  • Desktop keyboard control

    Every continuous control (knob, fader, XY pad, toggle, stutter) accepts keyboard nudges while the phone client has focus, so the desktop mapping panel and the phone UI share the same gesture model.

  • K-weighted loudness

    The AUD tab reports ITU-R BS.1770 K-weighted momentary, short-term and integrated loudness alongside the existing twelve descriptors.

The general snapshot runs at 30 Hz; audio descriptors have a separate immediate, bounded path. Mapping writes go through the SDK, while optional Deep Sync uses OSC UDP 11000/11001. Camera/audio media stays in the browser; numeric values cross the LAN. Browser buffers, Wi-Fi and Live scheduling add latency: measure your setup, with no universal or zero-delay guarantee.

AbletonOSC is needed only for Deep Sync. Without it the surface still works — pads, knobs, faders, sensors, mapping and snapshots are unaffected. It installs separately, is added in Live as a Control Surface, and requires UDP 11000 out and 11001 in to be free.

Long-press SYNC on the phone for Deep Sync Settings: clock source, subdivisions, phase offsets, swing, the shape used by LFOs and stutters, and the length of the pad mode D burst. The header button itself only toggles SYNC and FREE; the three-state SYNCED / SDK / FREE readout lives inside the transport overlay behind TRN.

The header — present on every page

BPM

Live's tempo, mirrored. Read-only.

Transport

Previous locator, play and pause, next locator. Controls Live's transport from the phone.

TRN

Full-screen overlay: PLAY, STOP, PREV, NEXT, REFRESH, a locator list and a search. Carries the SYNCED / SDK / FREE readout. Flashes green on beat 1, blue on the others.

MAP

Opens the mapping picker as an overlay on the current page. It has no tab of its own.

CFG

Per-control settings. Tap CFG, then the control: mode for a pad, shape and subdivision for an LFO, swing for a stutter, friction and bounce for XY 2. Each setting sticks to that control only. Long-press CFG to clear every override. PERF and MIX only; on the desktop, right-click the control instead.

SYNC

A two-state toggle: the button itself reads SYNC or FREE. Long-press for Deep Sync Settings. See 2.1.

Settings

Opens Deep Sync Settings directly, without the long-press.

CALIBRATE

Per page: SNS sets a stable neutral posture; AUD adjusts RMS/envelope response from five seconds of playing sound; VID checks lighting and hand tracking, using supported automatic camera settings. Separate states, cancel and reset.

STAGE

Locks the surface to the current page, preventing accidental page changes during performance.

Status

Link state and the phone's name. Tap to rename.

What each one does — from touch to Live

Each family below answers the same three questions: what your finger does, what the surface makes of it, and what reaches Live.

Choose a control family

Pads, LFOs, stutters

A starts at zero and returns there when the finger leaves; B starts from the saved value and keeps whatever you leave; C latches on a tap and can still be edited while held; D fires one attack–release burst. Choose the mode by what should happen after the touch, and set it per pad in CFG when one pad must behave differently. LFOs and stutters run on the same clock as the pads: locked to Live's beat in SYNC (through AbletonOSC or the SDK clock), free-running in FREE. PERF UTIL recalls bypass the SNP transition.

XY, knobs, faders

XY 2 records where and how fast you dragged, then coasts, rebounds off the edges and keeps sending after release; XY 1 stays where you leave it. Knobs and faders follow one rule: the value starts from where it is and tracks the vertical movement of the finger, never jumping to the touch point. A knob crosses its range in 150 px of drag (220 px for a macro); a fader thumb moves exactly as far as the finger does. They share one bank and the mapping surfaces, but each control keeps its own key and mapping.

Snapshots and vector morph

Capture stores every mapped control at once into one of eight slots. Recall does not jump: it moves each mapped value from where it is to the stored state across the transition, set in seconds (Free) or as a musical length that follows Live's tempo (Sync). Vector XY blends slots 1–4 by the position of one finger, weighting the four corners, and applies as you drag.

Vision: hand, poses, clutch

Direct hand X/Y/Z stays available for continuous control. A held, camera-facing pinch engages the clutch; hand displacement then drives its relative axes, and release freezes them. G1–G3 and the four built-in detectors are intentional outputs; a learned pose belongs to the hand that taught it, so teach with the hand you will perform with. PALM and FACE remain local camera diagnostics, not mapping traffic.

Phone MAP and panel Mappings edit the same source → transform → destination contract.

MIDI trigger notes
Map a phone control to a note on a MIDI track. The bundled RC-Midi-Receiver.amxd is reused when already on the track; otherwise place it manually because Live cannot insert Max for Live devices through its Extensions SDK. The Receiver arms SDK Notes by itself once it has loaded; Panic turns it off until clicked. Pads, LFOs, stutters, XY axes, knobs, faders, detectors, and learned poses support trigger-note targets.
Audio descriptors
AUD offers twelve descriptors plus amplitude controls. Kick and snare are attack-weighted heuristics inside their own bands, not instrument classifiers; low, mid and high are K-weighted loudness in LU. Microphone-to-Live latency depends on the phone and has not been measured end to end; Native Track is not part of 1.0, and the Max Audio Sender feeds the Receiver, not the browser descriptors.
TRN transport
A full-screen transport overlay reached from the header: play, stop, previous and next locator, refresh, locator search, and the SYNCED / SDK / FREE clock readout.
Deep Sync
Long-press SYNC for clock source, subdivisions, phase offsets, swing, the shapes used by LFOs and stutters, and the burst length for pad mode D. See 2.1.
Local HTTPS
A self-signed certificate is generated on first launch so the phone browser will grant camera and microphone permissions. Current LAN IPs are written into the certificate's SAN list.

Situations that make sense — who, what, and the mapping

These are complete starting points, not promises of a preset. In every case the source is named so the same result can be rebuilt in MAP.

Scenario A · two performers

A second pair of hands over one Live set

A soloist keeps playing while a colleague opens the phone. One finger moves an XY pad to shape the return effects without taking a controller away from the player.

xy-1.x→distortion Dry/Wetxy-1.y→reverb send

The phone is a second pair of hands on the same session: two continuous parameters, one gesture, no pause in the solo.

Scenario B · pianist

A pose becomes a sequencer cue

A pianist has both hands on the keys and cannot reach a pad. They capture three examples in G1 of a small, repeatable pose that can be made above the keyboard.

sensor.vision.gesture.1→MIDI trigger note

Each recognized pose sends a momentary 0→1 pulse to the mapped note, changing the accompanying pattern without asking the pianist to stop.

Scenario C · arrangement change

Four corners for four sections

A producer captures a sparse intro, verse, chorus, and outro into SNP slots 1–4. They switch to Vector XY and drag between the corners as the arrangement opens up.

knob-1→Auto Filter cutofffader-1→reverb return

The whole mapped surface follows the four-corner blend, with the transition time set to 2.0 s for a deliberate cross-section move.

Scenario D · pinch clutch

Push a filter in the air

A performer is standing with no table for a controller. They pinch with the palm facing the camera, wait for the clutch to engage, move their hand, and open the fingers to let go.

sensor.vision.pinch_z→Auto Filter cutoff

The cutoff moves from its current value by relative hand travel. Releasing the pinch freezes it there; a lost hand does not invent a new position.

Scenario E · instrument motion

The body supplies the modulation

A phone is secured to a guitar strap. The player's turn during a swell creates a repeatable rotation-rate gesture without adding a cable or a foot controller.

sensor.motion.gx→Echo feedback

The angular-motion channel becomes a live modulation source; CALIBRATE first establishes the instrument's neutral orientation.

Scenario F · acoustic input

Volume opens the effect

A singer or acoustic player leaves the phone near the microphone. Louder phrases should open a filter, while silence should close it, with no audio cable into Live.

sensor.audio.envelope→Saturator Drive

The smoothed loudness envelope follows the performance. Gate can be mapped separately when an on/off response is more useful than a continuous one.

6.0

Installation — from download to a mapped parameter

Steps 01 to 03 establish the link shown in 2.0. Nothing responds until the phone is connected.

Clip 1 Install and first pairing, narrated mp4

Install the extension

Use the .ablx supplied with this local candidate, or a versioned asset from Published releases. Double-click it to request installation in Live. Source version 1.0.0 alone does not mean a public v1.0.0 release exists.

If the Extensions menu does not appear, check that the Live 12.4.5+ Suite Extensions host is enabled, then restart Live.

Open the panel

Inside Live, open Extensions → RC Surface → Show panel. The panel shows a Performance QR code and the local connection status. The bridge normally reuses loopback HTTP 8730 and LAN HTTPS/WSS 8731; the panel shows the real URL if an override or occupied port forces a fallback.

Connect the phone

Scan the Performance QR code. Accept the self-signed HTTPS certificate warning once. The QR encodes the current URL. Hold the phone in landscape; portrait shows an orientation notice instead of the surface.

Pick a pad mode

The twelve pads take their behaviour from the mode bar: A momentary, B hold, C toggle, D burst. Move between PERF, MIX, SNP, SNS, AUD and VID to reach performance controls, knobs and faders, snapshots, sensors, audio and vision.

Arm the sensors

Enable Motion and orientation, Audio microphone analysis, or Vision single-hand tracking as needed. The browser asks for permission locally. Raw camera and microphone streams are never sent anywhere — only numeric control values leave the phone.

Map a control to Live

Tap MAP on the phone, select a highlighted control, then bind it to a Live parameter or to a MIDI trigger note. The picker groups Song, Main and Master, normal tracks, return tracks, devices and parameters, so the target context stays visible. Trigger notes use the bundled RC-Midi-Receiver.amxd.

Optional: Deep Sync

Install AbletonOSC separately and add it in Live as a Control Surface, keeping UDP 11000 and 11001 free. Long-press SYNC on the phone to pick the clock source. Without it the surface runs on the SDK BPM simulator or its own internal clock — see 2.1.

7.0

When it does not work

The failure modes are few and each has a single cause. Every entry names the condition that produced it, so the fix is a check, not a guess.

Troubleshooting

Phone cannot find the host

Same Wi-Fi. Live allowed through the firewall. Rescan the current QR.

The server prefers HTTP 8730 / HTTPS 8731 and falls back when busy. Tokens rotate on server Start; use the current QR after restart even if the port is unchanged.

AbletonOSC not detected

Install it separately as a Live Control Surface.

Keep UDP 11000 and 11001 free. Only Deep Sync needs it.

Vision will not start

Accept the certificate, grant camera, reload.

All three are required, in that order.

Audio is silent

Grant microphone, then tap the audio control again.

Browsers require a user gesture before capture starts.

No Extensions menu in Live

Check the Live 12.4.5+ Suite Extensions host is enabled, then restart Live.

Frequently asked — setup, limits, privacy

Which Live versions?

12.4.5+ Suite (Beta).

It is built on Live's native Extensions SDK. Live 11 and standard editions have no Extensions host.

Android and iOS?

iOS 15.4+ Safari, or Chromium on Android.

iOS 14.5 and below cannot get past local self-signed certificate warnings.

Does it need internet?

No. Everything runs on your local network.

The MediaPipe hand-tracking runtime ships with the extension and is served locally.

Can I map from the phone?

Yes. MAP, pick a control, then Bind or Trigger Note. Twelve AUD descriptor cards can modulate parameters.

The picker groups Song, Main and Master, tracks, returns, devices and parameters.

What about latency?

Local Wi-Fi and device processing both matter. General controls are coalesced at 30 Hz; audio has a separate bounded immediate path. End-to-end latency still needs measurement.

Router, distance, interference and host load all affect it. Measure on the target network.

Camera and microphone?

Processed inside the phone's browser. Never uploaded.

Only numeric control values reach Live, over the local WebSocket.

Official Ableton product?

No. Independent and source-available.

Not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Ableton AG.

Is AbletonOSC required?

Only for Deep Sync.

Without it the controller runs on the SDK BPM simulator or its own internal clock.

How mature is it?

v1.0.0. tsc --noEmit clean; the production build produces a working .ablx.

Smoke-tested in Live 12.4.5+ Suite (Beta) with a physical phone. The compatibility checklist remains published for additional OS, browser, and device combinations.
